## Name the building need

A request for better heating can mean warmer occupied rooms, more reliable domestic hot water, or both. Describe the affected use and locations in everyday language. Hydronic space heating and domestic hot water serve different purposes; the contractor needs to understand which service is involved before defining an assessment.

## Identify the interfaces

Buildings can have equipment and controls that connect several services. Owners should provide available system drawings and equipment records rather than assume that a boiler serves only one purpose. A qualified project team needs to confirm the actual arrangement and any interfaces before changing equipment or planning an interruption.

## Describe the occupancy

List the areas served, normal occupancy and known access restrictions. Explain whether the enquiry concerns a recurring service issue or a planned upgrade. Include observed timing and affected locations without guessing at temperatures, sizing or operating settings. This guide does not provide instructions for adjusting either system.

## Prepare a connected scope

Ask which services may be affected by the proposed work and who will coordinate notices, access and verification. The Haro project provides a related heating and hot water scope to discuss. Its equipment selection is specific to that project and should not be treated as a recommendation for another building.

## Planning checklist

- Affected service and locations
- Available drawings and equipment list
- Observed timing of the issue
- Access restrictions
- Responsibility for scope assessment
