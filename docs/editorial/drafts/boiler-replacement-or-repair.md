## Describe the problem before choosing the answer

Start with what building users have observed: areas that are not heating as expected, recurring interruptions, unusual sounds or changes in available hot water. Record when the issue occurs and whether it affects the whole building or particular areas. A symptom report helps frame an assessment; it does not establish the cause or justify a particular replacement.

## Bring the available history

Gather equipment identification, previous service reports, repair invoices and any existing drawings. Include outstanding recommendations and known changes to the building. If records are incomplete, say so. Photographs taken from a safe, accessible position can help identify equipment, but do not remove covers or enter restricted spaces to prepare an enquiry.

## Compare defined options

Ask what assessment is needed before comparing repair and replacement. Useful questions cover the proposed scope, connected systems, access, anticipated interruptions and responsibilities for design or specialist advice. Compare options against the same requirements rather than equipment price alone. Neither age nor a single fault is a universal decision rule.

## Agree the next decision

A useful next step may be a service visit, further investigation or a consultant-led review. Request a written explanation of what is known, what remains uncertain and what information is needed next. Ridgeview is a related boiler plant case, not proof that the same solution fits another building.

## Planning checklist

- Equipment identification and available reports
- Observed symptoms and dates
- Previous repairs and recommendations
- Access and occupancy restrictions
- Questions for the assessing specialist
