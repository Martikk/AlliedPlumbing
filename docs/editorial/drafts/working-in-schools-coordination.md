## Choose a clear school contact

Identify the people who can confirm access, scheduling constraints and changes to school use. Agree how questions and notices will be routed. The communication arrangement should suit the particular school and project, rather than rely on informal messages reaching everyone.

## Map the affected spaces

Discuss classrooms, corridors, service areas and delivery routes that may be affected. Ask which areas can be released and when. Site-specific safety and separation arrangements must be defined by the responsible team; do not infer approvals or universal credentials from another school project.

## Sequence around dependencies

Connect mechanical tasks with the supporting trades and the available work windows. The programme needs to account for the next activity as well as the current installation. Where afternoon, weekend or holiday work is proposed, confirm availability instead of presenting those hours as automatic.

## Close the communication loop

Agree how the school will learn about changes, readiness for use and outstanding work. Garibaldi and Leigh provide related mechanical project scopes. Their arrangements are examples for discussion, not a guarantee of identical access, timing or disruption at another site.

## Planning checklist

- School and contractor contacts
- Affected rooms and routes
- Agreed access windows
- Supporting trade dependencies
- Change notices and handover communication
